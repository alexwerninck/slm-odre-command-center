What H1, B1, B2, E1, E2, AF1, and AF2 mean

These are short internal names. Each card explains the customer job, why it matters, and opens the exact GoHighLevel workflow.

H1

H1: Paid Book Purchase, Access, and Routing

What it does: Starts when a paid Just Make The Phone Ring order is received. It records the buyer, requests book access, creates the customer opportunity, and currently sends the buyer into the legacy B1 workflow.

Why it matters: H1 is the first automation after a paid book order. Its B1 handoff must be corrected during the B1 cutover.

Open H1 in GoHighLevel
B1

B1: Book Buyer Follow-up

What it does: Welcomes book buyers and continues the sales follow-up toward the On-Demand Revenue Engine.

Why it matters: There are two versions because the protected version is a replacement built during migration. It is not a backup. The protected version adds Engine-owner checks so people stop receiving Engine sales messages after they buy or receive the Engine. The legacy version is still published, and H1 still sends buyers to it. Both versions also listen to the same paid book event, so one buyer can enter twice. The intended finish is one protected B1 as the only active version, with the legacy version kept as inactive history, not deleted.

Open protected B1Open legacy B1
B2

B2: Blueprint Call Booking Follow-up

What it does: Sends five emails and two SMS messages that move book buyers toward a Blueprint Call.

Why it matters: It stops pushing the call after the booked-call tag appears.

Open B2 in GoHighLevel
E1

E1: Blueprint Call Confirmation and Reminders

What it does: Starts from a confirmed OFFICIAL Blueprint Call and sends the booking confirmation, quiz prompts, and timed reminders.

Why it matters: It prepares the prospect for the call. The new S1 draft separately writes the Call Booked pipeline stage.

Open E1 in GoHighLevel
E2

E2: Blueprint Call No-show Recovery

What it does: Sends the follow-up that tries to recover a missed Blueprint Call.

Why it matters: The published E2 starts from the no-show tag. The new S2 draft will add that tag automatically from the OFFICIAL Blueprint Call No-show status and move the opportunity to In Chase. S2 still needs runtime testing and publication.

Open E2 in GoHighLevel
AF1

AF1: Partner Application Enrollment

What it does: Starts when the Partner application is submitted and enrolls the applicant in the correct Affiliate Manager campaign.

Why it matters: It begins the Partner journey, but it does not yet create the operating pipeline card.

Open AF1 in GoHighLevel
AF2

AF2: Partner Welcome and Link Delivery

What it does: Stores the Partner ID and application date, notifies Alex, and sends the welcome with personalized links, portal, toolkit, and onboarding call.

Why it matters: It delivers the Partner experience, but it does not yet move a Partner lifecycle pipeline card.

Open AF2 in GoHighLevel

What is good and what is risky

What is good

  • The customer-facing book, Engine, call, confirmation, partner, toolkit, and terms pages exist and returned HTTP 200.
  • The OFFICIAL Blueprint Call calendar is active and redirects to the correct confirmation page.
  • H1 already handles book ownership, Thinkific access, and an opportunity record.
  • B1, B2, E1, and E2 contain substantial follow-up instead of empty shells.
  • The Founding Students pipeline has the exact eight intended stages.
  • AF1 and AF2 create a working signup, Affiliate Manager enrollment, Partner ID, welcome, toolkit, and onboarding path.
  • The current AF2 Welcome V6 uses working personalized links and does not publish the stale commission table.

What is risky

  • Two published B1 workflows can react to the same paid book event.
  • The intended sales pipeline is nearly empty because current workflows write elsewhere or do not write opportunities.
  • Historical business assets in the account can make it easy to connect future work to the wrong place. They are excluded from current decisions.
  • A manual no-show tag means recovery depends on Alex remembering an administrative step.
  • The Partner journey has customer delivery but no lifecycle board.
  • The public toolkit contains internal comments in its source.
  • The email-template endpoint is unavailable, so this audit cannot certify every global email template.
